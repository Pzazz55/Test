# 1. Insurance Profitability Basics

## Combined Ratio

The **Combined Ratio** measures underwriting profitability by comparing claims and expenses to earned premiums.

### Formula

```text
Combined Ratio = (Incurred Losses + Expenses) / Earned Premium × 100

OR

Combined Ratio = Loss Ratio + Expense Ratio
```

Where:

```text
Loss Ratio = Claims / Earned Premium

Expense Ratio = Underwriting Expenses / Premium
```

### Interpretation

| Combined Ratio | Meaning |
|---------------|----------|
| < 100% | Underwriting Profit |
| = 100% | Break-even |
| > 100% | Underwriting Loss |

### Example

```text
Claims = $70M
Expenses = $20M
Earned Premium = $100M

Combined Ratio = (70 + 20) / 100 = 90%
```

Meaning:

```text
Insurer keeps $0.10 underwriting profit
for every $1 earned premium.
```

### Why It Matters

Evaluates:

- Underwriting discipline
- Pricing adequacy
- Expense management
- Risk management effectiveness

> Note: Combined Ratio excludes investment income.

# 2. Premium, Earned Premium & Unearned Premium

## Key Definitions

### Premium

Total premium charged for a policy.

### Earned Premium

Portion of premium corresponding to coverage already provided.

### Unearned Premium

Portion relating to future coverage still owed to policyholders.

---

## Example

Annual Premium:

```text
$1,200
```

After 3 months:

```text
Premium = $1,200
Earned Premium = $300
Unearned Premium = $900
```

---

## Earned Premium Formula

```text
Earned Premium =
Total Premium ×
(Coverage Elapsed ÷ Total Policy Term)
```

Example:

```text
$1,200 × 4/12 = $400
```

---

## Why Unearned Premium Matters

### Future Obligations

If a policy cancels, the insurer may refund the unearned portion.

### Revenue Recognition

Prevents overstating revenue.

### Future Revenue Pipeline

Large unearned premium reserves represent future revenue.

---

## Underwriting Profit Formula

```text
Underwriting Profit =
Earned Premium
− Claims
− Expenses
```

Example:

```text
Earned Premium = $100M
Claims = $60M
Expenses = $25M

Profit = $15M
```

---

## Insurance Revenue Flow

```text
Premium Collected
 ↓
Unearned Premium Liability
 ↓
Coverage Provided
 ↓
Earned Premium
 ↓
Claims & Expenses
 ↓
Underwriting Profit/Loss
```

# 3. Core Insurance KPIs

## Growth Metrics

### Gross Written Premium (GWP)

Total premium written before reinsurance.

```text
GWP = Total Premium Written
```

Measures:

- Company size
- Growth
- Market share

---

### Net Written Premium (NWP)

Premium retained after reinsurance.

```text
NWP =
Gross Premium
− Ceded Premium
```

Measures:

- Retained business
- Retained risk
- Exposure to future claims

---

## Profitability Metrics

### Loss Ratio

```text
Loss Ratio =
Claims ÷ Earned Premium
```

---

### Expense Ratio

```text
Expense Ratio =
Underwriting Expenses ÷ Premium
```

---

### Underwriting Margin

```text
Underwriting Margin =
100% − Combined Ratio
```

Example:

```text
Combined Ratio = 92%

Underwriting Margin = 8%
```

---

## Claims Metrics

### Claims Frequency

Measures how often claims occur.

```text
Frequency =
Number of Claims
÷
Number of Exposures
```

Example:

```text
2,000 claims
100,000 policies

Frequency = 2%
```

---

### Claims Severity

Measures average claim cost.

```text
Severity =
Total Claims Cost
÷
Number of Claims
```

Example:

```text
$50M losses
5,000 claims

Severity = $10,000
```

---

### Loss Cost

Core pricing metric:

```text
Loss Cost =
Frequency × Severity
```

---

## Solvency Metric

### Risk-Based Capital (RBC) Ratio

Measures capital strength relative to risk.

```text
RBC Ratio =
Available Capital
÷
Required Capital
```

### Interpretation

| RBC Ratio | Meaning |
|------------|----------|
| >300% | Strong |
| 200%-300% | Adequate |
| <200% | Regulatory Concern |
| Very Low | Possible Intervention |

# 4. Frequency, Severity & Pricing

## Core Pricing Equation

```text
Expected Loss Cost =
Frequency × Severity
```

---

## Frequency Drivers

Examples:

- Driver behavior
- Fraud
- Safety controls
- Weather
- Exposure levels

---

## Severity Drivers

Examples:

- Repair inflation
- Medical inflation
- Litigation costs
- Catastrophe losses
- Vehicle technology costs

---

## Why Severity Matters

Example:

```text
100 claims × $8,000 = $800K

100 claims × $10,500 = $1.05M
```

Losses rise by:

```text
31.25%
```

Premiums usually need to increase accordingly.

---

## Insurer Actions

### To Reduce Frequency

- Better underwriting
- Telematics
- Driver coaching
- Safety programs
- Fraud detection

### To Reduce Severity

- Higher deductibles
- Claims cost control
- Repair networks
- Coverage modifications
- Reinsurance

---

# 5. Reinsurance

## What Is Reinsurance?

Insurance purchased by insurers to transfer part of their risk.

---

## Benefits

### Improves Solvency

Protects against:

- Hurricanes
- Wildfires
- Earthquakes
- Cyber catastrophes
- Large liability claims

---

### Improves RBC

Example:

```text
Capital = $1B

Required Capital:
Before = $500M
After = $300M

RBC:
200% → 333%
```

---

### Reduces Earnings Volatility

Creates more stable financial results.

---

### Supports Growth

Allows insurers to write more business without raising equivalent capital.

---

## Costs

### Direct Costs

- Reinsurance premium
- Administration costs

### Risks

- Counterparty risk
- Capacity risk
- Basis risk
- Profit leakage
- Reinsurance pricing inflation

---

## Reinsurer Selection Criteria

1. Financial strength
2. Claims-paying reputation
3. Industry expertise
4. Capacity
5. Pricing
6. Diversification value
7. Strategic support

---

## Reinsurer Diversification Best Practices

- Use multiple reinsurers
- Diversify geographically
- Diversify by credit quality
- Diversify by contract type
- Set concentration limits
- Stress-test recoveries

# 6. Telematics

## What Is It?

Technology that captures driving behavior.

Examples:

- Speeding
- Braking
- Mileage
- Phone usage
- Night driving

---

## How It Reduces Frequency

### Better Risk Selection

Identifies safer versus riskier drivers.

### Behavior Modification

Drivers tend to improve behavior when monitored.

### Incentive-Based Discounts

Rewards safer behavior.

### Lower Exposure

Accurately tracks mileage.

---

## How It Affects Pricing

Traditional pricing relied on:

- Age
- ZIP Code
- Vehicle type
- Driving history

Telematics pricing uses:

```text
Actual Driving Behavior
```

Result:

- Better segmentation
- More accurate pricing
- Lower adverse selection
- Better loss ratios

---

## Telematics Pricing Workflow

```text
Driving Data
 ↓
Risk Features
 ↓
Frequency Model
 ↓
Severity Model
 ↓
Expected Loss Cost
 ↓
Premium
```

# 11. PML, AAL & EP Curves

## PML (Probable Maximum Loss)

Largest reasonably foreseeable loss.

Example:

```text
TIV = $100M
PML = $25M
```

---

## MPL (Maximum Possible Loss)

Assumes extreme failure conditions.

May approach:

```text
Total Loss
```

---

## AAL (Average Annual Loss)

Expected annual loss averaged across all scenarios.

---

## EP Curve (Exceedance Probability Curve)

Shows the probability losses exceed a given threshold.

Example:

```text
10% chance loss exceeds $10M
5% chance loss exceeds $25M
1% chance loss exceeds $50M
0.4% chance loss exceeds $100M
```

Used for:

- Reinsurance pricing
- Capital allocation
- Premium setting
- Attachment point decisions
